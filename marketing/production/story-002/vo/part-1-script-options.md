## Option A - quiet confession (124 words)

I lied on my resume. It was one line. I wrote that I had run social media for
a real company, when the truth is I had run one page for my cousin's bakery.
They called me the next morning. I sat in that interview and watched them nod
at a person who did not exist, and then I shook their hands and took the job
anyway. So I did the only thing left. I stayed up every night learning the
work I had claimed to know, terrified that someone would ask me the one
question I could not answer. Three months later my manager stopped me in the
hallway. She said she had never seen numbers like mine. And then she said the
word promotion.

## Option B - the lie that grew (118 words)

Nobody checks one line on a resume. That is what I told myself the night I
typed a job I never had into the middle of mine. And it worked. Two
interviews, one offer, a desk with my name on it. But a lie does not stay one
line. In every meeting I nodded along at words I was googling under the
table, and every night I studied until the fake version of me and the real one
started to blur. Here is the cruel part. I got good. Genuinely good. So good
that on Friday my boss closed her office door, smiled, and told me the company
wanted to promote me. All I could hear was the lie.
