## The category's actual growth pattern

**From-memory:** The winning anonymous products were not mainly anonymous
outbound mail products. A person first created a public reply surface, usually
a personal link in an Instagram or Snapchat Story. Their friends wrote replies.
The owner then reposted selected replies, exposing the same link to another
group of prospective senders. The receiver was the distributor.

Beatass starts differently: one sender writes and plays, then one recipient
gets an email with the confession and doll GIF. This is lower-friction for the
sender because the recipient did not first need to claim a profile link. It
also means the historical profile-link loop does not occur automatically. The
recipient has to choose to share the unusual email or its doll video, and that
choice has to lead to a simple new sending experience.

## App-by-app evidence and takeaways

### NGL

- **From-memory, viral mechanic:** A user posted an NGL link to an Instagram
  Story asking followers to answer anonymously. Incoming messages were easy to
  screenshot or repost to Stories, where the personal link appeared again. The
  loop was attention first, then curiosity, then a low-effort reply.
- **From-memory, failure/limit:** The FTC alleged NGL sent computer-generated
  messages that appeared to come from friends and used deceptive paid identity
  "hints". This is the clearest hard boundary for Beatass. The FTC announced a
  $5 million payment and a prohibition on serving people under 18 in its 2024
  action. Recheck the legal status and exact order language before quoting it.
- **Transfer:** The doll video, not a generic text screenshot, is Beatass's
  repostable proof of play. It needs a small, legible `beatass.com` watermark
  in the exported 9:16 MP4. The message itself must always be real user input.
  Do not add fake starter confessions, fake inbox counts, fake "someone is
  thinking about you" nudges, or paid sender-identification hints.

### Sarahah

- **From-memory, viral mechanic:** The service centered on a personal feedback
  URL. Snapchat's easier external-link sharing in 2017 gave people a natural
  place to put it, and a recipient's desire to see what friends "really"
  thought drove posting. It was an inbound, receiver-consented loop.
- **From-memory, failure/limit:** Abuse reports and privacy concerns destroyed
  trust. Apple and Google removed the app after a prominent safety campaign.
  Recheck the dates, removal reason, and later shutdown status before citing.
- **Transfer:** A web-first tool removes the install step, but never removes
  the need for a safe sharing surface. Beatass should make one great video
  export and one obvious, honest creation link instead of asking for contact
  permissions or treating a recipient's address as a marketing list.

### Sendit

- **From-memory, viral mechanic:** Sendit gave Snapchat users interactive
  prompts and an answer link to put on a Story. A game or question supplied a
  repeatable reason to repost, rather than relying on a one-time confession.
- **From-memory, failure/limit:** This part of the category faces the same
  anonymous harassment risk as Sarahah and NGL. Public litigation has alleged
  fake or automated questions in Sendit. Treat that claim as an allegation
  unless a court record or final regulator finding is obtained. Do not repeat
  it as fact in launch content.
- **Transfer:** Make recurring content prompts for Beatass marketing, such as
  fictional "the group chat finally snapped" skits. In product, every actual
  confession must originate with a sender, with no automated filler presented
  as a person.

### Gas

- **From-memory, viral mechanic:** Gas limited early use to a familiar school
  graph. Positive polls about classmates created an immediate reward for the
  subject and a notification reason to come back. That is a network-density
  tactic, not a public link tactic.
- **From-memory, failure/limit:** Discord acquired Gas in January 2023 and
  announced its shutdown in November 2023. It therefore cannot be used as
  evidence that its growth model remained durable after the initial launch.
- **Transfer:** Use familiar situations as voluntary creative prompts: a flat
  mate, an ex, a group project, a bad first date. Do not use school seeding,
  contacts, address books, or location to infer who a person knows. Consider a
  clearly optional "love" or apology creative mode only if it stays within the
  existing product's safety decisions.

### Whisper

- **From-memory, viral mechanic:** Whisper converted secrets into a visual
  quote card, then distributed them through topic, group, and nearby feeds.
  The visual card was easy to understand before reading a profile or installing
  a social graph.
- **From-memory, failure/limit:** The service faced persistent moderation and
  privacy criticism. The Guardian reported location-tracking concerns in 2023.
  That reporting should be read in full, together with the company's response,
  before making a legal or technical claim.
- **Transfer:** Beatass can use an immediately comprehensible visual artifact:
  doll action, short line of confession, damage stat, small URL. Do not create
  an anonymous public feed, a map, or a nearby feature. A share happens only
  after the sender or recipient chooses it.

### Yik Yak

- **From-memory, viral mechanic:** A local anonymous wall made every post
  relevant to people physically near one another. College concentration,
  voting, and a live common conversation made the app a daily ritual.
- **From-memory, failure/limit:** The original team said it would wind down in
  2017. The documented category lesson is the difficulty of moderating abuse,
  threats, and bullying in a high-context anonymous community. Do not infer
  current product status from the original shutdown.
- **Transfer:** Its strength was a specific social situation, not anonymity by
  itself. Beatass content can name situations, but the product must not publish
  recipient-targeted confessions to a public or local group. No college
  ambassador program aimed at minors.

### Tellonym

- **From-memory, viral mechanic:** Tellonym gave each person a durable profile
  URL and an inbox for anonymous "tells." Users posted the link on Instagram
  or Snapchat and reposted selected answers, creating the same owner-led
  inbound loop as NGL and Sarahah.
- **From-memory, failure/limit:** Youth use, anonymous bullying, and content
  moderation are persistent constraints. The live status of the app and its
  current safety controls were not verified here.
- **Transfer:** A claimed Beatass inbox link might be a later, explicit opt-in
  feature. It should not be required for the first send. Until it exists, the
  email recipient sees a generic invitation to create their own doll, not a
  personal profile link created without their consent.

## The sender-only email loop to test

The viable loop is not "recipient receives an email, therefore viral." It is:

1. A sender makes a real confession and a doll video.
2. The recipient receives the full confession and GIF, with prominent report
   and permanent block controls.
3. If the recipient enjoys or wants to react to it, they voluntarily share the
   branded video or tell someone about the site.
4. Their friend opens a generic Beatass creation page and becomes the next
   sender.

The product can make step 3 easy. It cannot fake, demand, or assume it.

### Email changes

- Keep the full user-written confession inline, as already decided. Do not
  switch it to a teaser or fake a new-message notification.
- Put the small animated GIF in the email and make the brand readable on the
  first frame and the last frame: `beatass.com`. This is an origin label, not
  a claim that other people are using it.
- After the content, use one plain secondary CTA: **"Want to say it with a
  doll? Make one at beatass.com."** It should go to the generic creator, not a
  page containing the recipient's address, name, or message.
- Give the recipient an optional, privacy-aware route to the shareable 9:16
  video. Do not publish it on a public URL by default. If a hosted result page
  is added, protect it with an unguessable link and make download/share a
  deliberate recipient action.
- Keep **"Report this"** and **"Block my address forever"** equally visible or
  more visible than the marketing CTA. Never email the recipient follow-up
  promotion after they block or unsubscribe.
- Candidate audience-facing subject, to test only after deliverability review:
  **"[Name], someone made you a doll."** It is specific, truthful, and avoids
  invented social proof. Do not use deceptive urgency or "your friends" copy.

### Result-page changes

- Put the 1080x1920 MP4 at the center, with a real **Share** button that opens
  the phone's native share sheet and a **Save video** fallback. The sender has
  already played; do not make them re-encode or re-upload.
- Burn the short confession, doll stats, and `beatass.com` into the shared
  video. The readable artifact is the recruiting surface.
- Offer an editable suggested caption, never an automatic post: **"I said it
  with a doll. Your turn: beatass.com"**. Make the default neutral enough that
  a user can remove it without losing the file.
- Add **"Make another"** immediately after sharing. Do not show invented
  counters, testimonials, or claims that a recipient has viewed the email.
- Use aggregate referral tags only after a privacy review. Do not put recipient
  email addresses, message text, names, or predictable IDs in share URLs.

### What not to copy from this category

- Do not make or seed fictional product messages and then present them as user
  confessions, testimonials, or proof of traffic.
- Do not add a paid "who sent this?" feature, a hint, approximate location, or
  device information.
- Do not import contacts, scrape follower lists, or send a marketing sequence
  to people who receive a confession.
- Do not create a public feed, local wall, school campaign, or any campaign
  designed to reach minors.
- Do not use bought followers, automated engagement, engagement pods, or
  undisclosed automated messages. The only approved automated Instagram DM
  notifier remains disclosed, exact-handle, block-list honoring, and capped at
  30 per day.

## Minimum experiments and decision rules

Track events only where the person has actually acted: `send_completed`,
`result_shared`, `video_saved`, `email_cta_opened`, and `referral_send_completed`.
Do not convert any of these into fake public counters.

| Experiment | Compare | Scale when | Kill or change when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Result-page CTA | Native Share first versus Save first | The Share-first version produces a meaningfully higher actual share rate without more support or abuse reports | Share rate does not improve after enough real completions to make direction clear |
| Email CTA wording | "Make one" versus "Say it with a doll" | More recipients become first-time senders, with no higher block/report rate | A stronger CTA raises blocks/reports or shows no referral lift |
| Export watermark | Small end-card URL versus readable always-on URL | Referral sends rise while completion/share rate stays steady | The watermark makes people abandon or stops saving/sharing |

No numeric threshold is stated until a real baseline exists. Set it from the
first clean cohort, using actual event data, then record the threshold before
the next experiment. A complaint, block, or abuse increase is a stop signal,
not a conversion cost to optimize away.
