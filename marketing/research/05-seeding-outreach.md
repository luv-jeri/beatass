## Non-negotiable guardrails

- Never fake a product message, user count, testimonial, comment, or creator reaction.
- Fictional confession stories are allowed as labelled entertainment content. They are not user proof.
- Never buy followers, likes, comments, views, or shares. Never use engagement pods.
- Do not contact a person again after a no, an unsubscribe, a block, or no response to one permission-first message.
- Do not ask creators to hide a material connection. If a creator receives payment, a gift, exclusive access, or anything else of value, they must use the platform's disclosure tools and make a clear disclosure.
- Do not use automated accounts to find, follow, comment on, or message prospects.

## Who to seed

Build a 30-person prospect list manually, not through scraping. A good first-pass prospect:

- makes short-form comedy, fictional POV, relationship humor, office humor, pop-culture memes, or animation
- appears to have an adult audience and does not make minors its core audience
- has a recent posting habit, visible real comments, and a tone that can carry dark but non-abusive comedy
- has 5,000-100,000 followers or a smaller account with unusually high comment quality
- already uses Reels, TikTok remixes, duets, stitches, green-screen commentary, or fan prompt formats
- has public business-contact permission or a demonstrated openness to creator pitches
- has no recent history of harassment, targeting private people, hate content, or fabricated confession content

For each prospect, track only what is necessary: handle, platform, audience fit, one specific recent post, date of first contact, reply, permission state, and "do not contact" state. Do not store private personal data, scraped email lists, or speculation about age, identity, or income.

## Permission-first outreach templates

Use a human sender. Send only one first message. Replace every bracket with a real observation. Do not automate these messages.

### Creator DM, first contact

Hi [name], I make beatass.com, a free little web toy where someone writes an anonymous confession, beats up a voodoo doll, and gets a shareable video. Your [specific recent post] made me think the fictional-confession format might fit your audience.

This is a one-time message from the maker, not an automated campaign. Would you like a link to try it? No pressure, and I will not follow up if it is not for you.

### Creator DM, after they say yes

Thanks. Here is the link: https://beatass.com

The useful angle is a clearly fictional POV or "unsent message" skit, not pretending a confession came from a real user. If you post because we gave you anything beyond the free public product, please use the platform's paid-partnership or branded-content disclosure. If you want, I can send three fictional prompt ideas.

### Business-email pitch, only where a public pitch address permits it

Subject: A small fictional-confession video idea for [creator name]

Hi [name],

I am [name] from beatass.com. I saw that you invite business pitches at this address. We made a free web toy that turns a fictional anonymous confession into a voodoo-doll video. I thought it could fit your [specific series or post].

Would you like the link and three made-for-your-format prompt ideas? If not, reply "no" and I will not contact you again.

[real sender name]
[business postal contact method]
[simple opt-out instruction]

Do not use this template until the sender has a truthful postal contact method and the message is checked against current law and the creator's stated contact preference.

### Meme-page message

Hi [name], I run beatass.com. Your page's [specific format] gave me a dumb but potentially good remix idea: a clearly fictional "the message I would never send" card, then a voodoo doll reaction.

This is a one-time human message, not an automated blast. Want the tool link and a sample prompt? Totally fine if not.

## Duet and stitch bait that stays on the right side

Use simple prompts that let the viewer be funny without naming or targeting a real person:

1. "Stitch this with the 7-word message you would never send. Fictional answers only."
2. "Duet as the voodoo doll's lawyer. What is its defense?"
3. "POV: the group chat voted and the doll lost."
4. "Finish this sentence: 'I am not mad. I just made a doll because...' "
5. "Use this sound with a fictional office grievance. No names, no screenshots."

Build each video like this:

1. First second: state the prompt in large on-screen text.
2. Second 1-4: show the doll reacting.
3. Second 4-7: show the fictional card or the creator's line.
4. Final second: "Make a fictional one at beatass.com" plus a clear "fictional example" label where needed.

Do not reuse an outside creator's footage without the platform's available remix permission and the creator's consent where required. Do not use a trending sound outside the platform library or outside its licence.

## Email and DM operating limits

The FTC CAN-SPAM guide is aimed at commercial email. Its remembered core requirements include truthful routing and subject information, identifying an ad where required, a valid postal address, a working opt-out, and prompt honouring of an opt-out. Verify the exact current text at the FTC URL above. The safer operational rule here is simpler:

- No bulk email.
- No scraped contacts.
- One highly relevant permission-first email at most.
- No follow-up unless the person opted in or replied.
- Keep a permanent do-not-contact list.
- Use a real sender name and a working reply path.

For Instagram, the existing approved recipient notifier remains capped at 30 per day and must say that it is automated. New creator outreach stays manual and small. There is no daily number that guarantees safety; relevance, consent, repetition, account health, and current platform enforcement all matter.

## Experiment scorecard

Track each public post and each permitted outreach contact separately:

- Post: reach, average watch time, completion rate, shares per reach, saves per reach, profile visits, outbound clicks, tool starts, completed sends, native shares.
- Creator outreach: contacts sent, permission replies, tool tries, voluntary posts, reach, completed sends, and blocks or complaints.
- Safety: report rate, block rate, abuse terms caught, delivery complaints, and platform warnings.

Kill an outreach format immediately if it creates a complaint, a block pattern, a platform warning, repeated negative replies, or a materially worse report rate. Scale a content format only after at least three separate posts show above-account-baseline shares per reach and produce measurable tool starts. Scale by making more original variations, not by increasing unsolicited contact volume.
