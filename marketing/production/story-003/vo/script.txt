A storm washed out the trail, and by eleven at night a lost hiker was walking in circles with a dying flashlight. Then he saw a light between the trees. It was a small cottage, one candle in the window, and nobody home. Inside there was a single room, with a bed in the middle, and a table of warm food, as if someone had cooked it minutes ago. He waited two hours under twelve paintings of smiling people, four on every wall, their eyes wide open, watching him. He told himself it was nothing. So he ate the food, lay down on the bed, and slept. At sunrise he grabbed his bag and ran, and he never stopped running. Because those were never paintings. They were windows.
