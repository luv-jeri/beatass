I spent the night in a diner that burned down forty years ago. My car died in a thunderstorm, but a red OPEN sign led me into a warm booth. A smiling waitress poured coffee while eight silent customers sat facing the windows. Each lightning flash showed reflections facing the wrong way. Then all eight heads snapped toward me at once. At sunrise, firefighters dragged me from beneath the table. Wet ashes and twisted metal surrounded us. The diner burned down in 1986. Eight customers were never found.
