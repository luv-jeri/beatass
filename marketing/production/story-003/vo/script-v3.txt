A hiker got lost in the mountains one night, and by eleven his flashlight was dying. Then he saw a light between the trees, a small cottage with one single candle in the window. He knocked three times, nobody answered, and the door swung open on its own. Inside there was one room, with a bed in the middle and a table of warm food, still steaming, as if the cook had just vanished. Then he noticed the walls. Twelve paintings hung around him, every face smiling, every eye wide open, and every single one of them faced the bed. He waited two hours, and nobody came. So he ate, he lay down under those smiling faces, and somehow he slept. At sunrise he tore out of that cottage, and he never stopped running. Because those were never paintings. They were windows. And they were never empty.
